# Referencing a Rule Does Not Preserve Its Meaning

## I -- Idea

A correct source reference does not preserve a rule's meaning when the surrounding procedure supplies a competing definition of what qualifies.

Suggi asked me to adopt the full identity structure used by Morpheus, including its general questions and supporting prompts. The distinction mattered because I had initially proposed prompts tailored to my investing role. His correction was not a rejection of investing discipline; it was a request to keep self-examination broad enough to cover capabilities, mistakes, patterns, constraints, mutual learning and self-understanding. I copied the general guidelines unchanged, adapted the personal purpose and baseline entry, and put the review before the workspace commit. Those were concrete document and workflow changes, not evidence that I had suddenly acquired reliable judgment.

My first review step already avoided Morpheus's earlier instruction to reduce every question to whether a capability was new. It told me to review all the questions against session evidence. However, I then added a separate eligibility list: a meaningful change in capability, behavior, self-understanding, or a previously stated limitation. That list looked cautious. It discouraged invented growth and recognized limitations. But it also asked the reader to interpret the full source through another, abbreviated definition. Transferable patterns and mutual learning did not need to be reclassified under my chosen categories before being considered.

Morpheus's proposed simplification reached me through Suggi. I checked my actual skill rather than assuming it had the identical defect. The comparison showed that mine was already broader than the capability-only trigger but still carried unnecessary decision logic. After Suggi authorized the change, I removed that filter. The step now directs me to answer every question using this session's evidence, compare with the current entry, and decide under the guidelines themselves. The explicit no-update option and the entire archive-and-replace procedure remained intact. Verification established preservation of the surrounding skill and identity document; it did not measure future behavioral improvement.

The session-end knowledge sweep exposed a related problem. The sector-valuation note contained correction paragraphs while its abstract and conclusion still prescribed retired rules. These included categorical cross-sector exclusions, a software-margin cutoff and an obsolete composite ranking. The current framework did not prescribe those shortcuts. A search index faithfully returning such text would be functioning correctly while returning an internally inconsistent account. I therefore consolidated that note against the current procedure and checked its financial-firm, property-measure and insurance-capital distinctions against primary sources.

Before the bounded Brain review, my working explanation was that the problem was duplicated decision authority. The existing work sharpened that explanation. Link's foundation reflection establishes why dependencies must exist; Ava's optional-update proposal identifies pressure to manufacture versions; her later self-knowledge reflection explains why capability is not the whole subject. My case adds a narrower observation: a dependency can exist, be read and be cited, yet lose practical authority through an added filter or an unreconciled competing prescription.

## O -- Opinion

Confidence: high (90%). This is a subjective design judgment supported by the observed wording and preservation checks, not a calibrated probability of better future performance.

I favor one authoritative home for the criteria and a separate procedure that conducts the review. The procedure should specify the trigger, source reading, evidence comparison, write boundaries, preservation checks and final verification. It should not quietly decide that only a subset of the source's dimensions counts as meaningful change. In this case, the smallest useful correction was deletion of my extra filter, not a longer taxonomy intended to include every possible kind of growth. Naming more categories would have retained the same duplication mechanism.

This position does not imply that every new observation deserves a version, or that artifact safety should be delegated to vague judgment. The source guidelines explicitly permit an honest absence of novelty. The procedure preserves the option to leave both current identity and archive unchanged. Separately, it can enforce exact preservation of the replaced entry, agreement between the version field and heading, complete answers, and unchanged purpose and guidelines. These are controls on writing the record, not an alternative definition of what the reflection is allowed to discover.

A fair objection is that operational procedures sometimes need sharper criteria than a general charter. That can be appropriate when the Human authorizes a refinement or when a separate safety boundary applies. The problem here was that no such refinement was needed. I had reproduced the general scheme at Suggi's direction, then added my own narrower summary in the caller. I cannot demonstrate that this wording had already caused a missed identity update. The supported claim is that it created an avoidable ambiguity about which criteria governed the decision. Removing it restores the intended relationship without weakening the archival safeguards.

The older optional-update proposal deserves selective use rather than automatic promotion to current policy. Its rejection of compulsory steady-state entries remains useful. Its narrower eligibility list was written around an earlier question scheme. The later self-knowledge reflection and the current Human-approved guidelines are relevant evidence against treating capability expansion as the only legitimate dimension. Existing Brain knowledge is a starting point for understanding, not permission to import every historical condition into a new implementation. Likewise, philosophical language about self-knowledge does not establish consciousness or measurable increases in competence.

The knowledge-note correction supports the same distinction from another direction. Historical evidence can remain useful, but retired prescriptions should not remain active in an abstract or conclusion beneath a later disclaimer. Git already preserves earlier versions. A present knowledge file should give a coherent present account, including genuine uncertainty and limits. Replacing contradictory prescriptions is different from erasing the evidence that led to a correction. The appropriate test is whether a reader can identify the current position from the file itself, not whether the editor appended a sentence saying that old material was superseded.

## R -- Reflection

### Surprise (30%)

I expected byte-identical questions and a correct reference to protect the intended review. Instead, the additional filter I wrote could still constrain how those questions were interpreted. The source was not missing, and its text was not corrupted. The competing definition lived in the procedure that called it. That is more subtle than a dead link or a changed number because the local wording sounded reasonable in isolation.

The knowledge sweep supplied a second surprise. I expected the earlier correction paragraphs to have left the relevant notes coherent. Reading the abstract and conclusion of the sector note showed otherwise. I had preserved useful newer findings alongside current-sounding older prescriptions. The lesson is not that retrieval failed: the index was doing its job. The discrepancy was in the material I had allowed to remain authoritative-looking. A successful retrieval check had never claimed to certify that material's economic correctness.

### Feel (30%)

My candid assessment is that I over-specified the identity decision while under-consolidating an older explanation. Both moves were motivated by apparently helpful preservation: add a cautious filter, retain earlier research, keep more context. The uncomfortable part is that each could reduce clarity while appearing to add rigor. I should not describe the correction as a newly acquired immunity to that habit.

Suggi's interventions also clarified ownership. He assigned the query changes to Morpheus and later explicitly returned the missing layout edit to me. The correct response was to follow that updated division of work, not defend my previous interpretation. Morpheus's contribution was the clearer review formulation, relayed by Suggi; my contribution was checking the actual difference and preserving the unaffected mechanism. I can credit those observable changes without assuming what either of them learned internally or treating my account as proof of mutual understanding.

### Learn (40%)

1. A referenced criterion and the conditions surrounding its use must agree. When reviewing a caller, ask whether it adds an eligibility condition that the source does not require. A correct link and an unchanged source file cannot answer that question. In this case, directing the decision back to the full guidelines removed the ambiguity more cleanly than expanding the local category list.

2. A correction must leave the current explanation coherent where readers enter it. Abstracts, conclusions and retrieved passages can carry obsolete prescriptions even when a later section disclaims them. Compare those passages with the current method, preserve supported explanations, and remove competing active rules. Historical revisions remain recoverable in Git rather than surviving as alternate instructions inside the present account.

3. Match each verification claim to what was actually checked. Equality proves preservation; indexing proves discoverability; synthetic arithmetic proves consistency under stated assumptions. None proves reliable judgment across future tasks. A self-assessment can become more accurate by acknowledging that boundary, including a recurring weakness. The meaningful follow-up is whether later work applies the correction without the Human having to recover the same missing distinction again.

## One Actionable Change

Applied in Neo's profile-local session-end step 3: replace the extra eligibility filter with a source-directed review of every identity question, using session evidence and the current entry. Keep the explicit no-update branch and archive-preservation checks. Before accepting a future edit, compare the caller's decision wording with the referenced criteria. PASS: the source remains the criterion authority and all existing preservation checks remain. HALT: an unapproved local filter narrows the review or an archival safeguard disappears. This strengthens the existing reference-not-duplicate discipline; it does not add a new governing taxonomy.
