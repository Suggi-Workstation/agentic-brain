# Business-management-strategy

## Anchor

The study of how an individual organization creates, captures, and
compounds value through its business model, strategy, management,
operations, governance, culture, and execution. This domain treats the
company as an operating system: how resources become products and
services, how customers are acquired and retained, how incentives and
decisions flow through the organization, and why some firms improve
while others decay. It bridges the external playing field of industry
analysis with the internal reality an owner or manager must understand.

## Scope

**In:** business-model design, unit economics, pricing power and
revenue models, customer acquisition and retention, sales and
distribution, marketing strategy, operating leverage, cost structure,
process design and operational excellence, supply and procurement
strategy, organizational design, decentralization and delegation,
management systems, culture and incentives, corporate governance,
board effectiveness, executive compensation, succession planning,
principal-agent problems, innovation management, competitive strategy
at the firm level, resource allocation, mergers as strategy,
turnarounds, crisis management, quality systems, execution discipline,
and methods for evaluating business quality.

**Out:** industry-wide structure and profit pools (industries-sectors),
financial statement mechanics and capital structure (finance),
investment philosophy (value-investing), biographies of business
leaders (notable-people), specific company events analyzed as cases
(case-studies), psychological mechanisms without organizational
application (psychology-behavior), detailed corporate law
(law-regulation), and technical engineering or product implementation
(engineering-infrastructure and technology).
