# Investment-vehicles-fund-structures

## Anchor

The study of the legal, economic, governance, and capital structures
through which investment assets are owned, pooled, managed, and
compounded. Encompasses permanent-capital holding companies such as
Berkshire Hathaway, insurance-float compounders, investment companies,
mutual funds, ETFs, closed-end funds, investment trusts, hedge funds,
private-equity and venture-capital partnerships, family offices, BDCs,
and REITs as ownership vehicles. The anchor is structure: how the
vehicle's funding permanence, incentives, liquidity promises, fees,
tax treatment, governance, and ability to allocate capital shape its
long-term results.

## Scope

**In:** permanent-capital holding companies, Berkshire-style capital
allocation structures, insurance float as investable funding,
conglomerate and investment-company design, mutual funds and ETFs,
closed-end funds and investment trusts, hedge-fund and investment
partnership structures, private-equity and venture-capital funds,
family offices, business development companies, REITs as investment
vehicles, management companies and carried interest, fee structures
and incentive alignment, redemption and forced-selling risk,
liquidity mismatches, fund leverage, governance and shareholder
rights, tax and regulatory structure at the vehicle level, fund
lifecycle and succession, permanent vs redeemable capital, and
comparative analysis of vehicle architecture.

**Out:** biographies of individual investors (investors), abstract
investment philosophy (value-investing), valuation of underlying
securities (valuation-screening), portfolio construction mathematics
(portfolio-risk-management), general capital-market mechanics
(finance), detailed legal doctrine (law-regulation), operating-company
strategy (business-management-strategy), and one-off investment events
analyzed mainly for lessons (case-studies).
