# Sociology-demography

## Anchor

The study of social structure, institutions, group relationships, and
population change in modern and historical societies. Sociology
examines how class, status, organizations, norms, networks, inequality,
and institutions shape collective life; demography measures how
fertility, mortality, migration, age structure, and household formation
change societies over time. The anchor is the social level of analysis:
patterns that emerge above the individual and across populations.

## Scope

**In:** social institutions, class and stratification, inequality and
social mobility, race and ethnicity as social systems, gender and
family structures, organizations and professions, social networks and
social capital, community and civil society, labor and the sociology
of work, education as an institution, religion as a social institution,
crime and deviance as social phenomena, collective behavior, social
movements, urbanization, migration, fertility and mortality,
population aging, household formation, demographic transition,
population projections, survey and census methods, and theories of
social change and modernization.

**Out:** cross-cultural ethnography and premodern cultural systems
(anthropology), individual cognition and behavior
(psychology-behavior), formal government and policy design
(political-science-public-policy), broad chronological narrative
(history), economic theory (macro-micro), clinical treatment and
epidemiology (health-medicine), and rhetoric or media-production
technique (communication).
