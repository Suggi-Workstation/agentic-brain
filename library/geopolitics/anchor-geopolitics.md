# Geopolitics

## Anchor

The study of how geography, power, economics, and culture shape
relations between nations and global actors. Encompasses
international relations theory, diplomatic history, conflict and
security studies, trade dynamics, energy geopolitics, and the
strategic competition between great powers. This domain is about
the chessboard of global power -- who has it, how they use it,
and what happens when it shifts.

## Scope

**In:** international relations theory (realism, liberalism,
constructivism), great power competition, diplomatic history,
conflict and war studies, energy geopolitics, trade wars and
sanctions, alliance systems (NATO, etc.), international
institutions (UN, WTO, IMF), nuclear proliferation, cyber
warfare and security, territorial disputes, resource competition,
soft power and cultural influence, intelligence and espionage,
decolonization and post-colonial dynamics.

**Out:** domestic politics of specific countries, economic theory
(macro-micro), military technology (technology), international
law specifics (law-regulation), historical events without
geopolitical analysis (case-studies), travel writing.

## Adjacent domains

- **macro-micro**: economic dimensions of geopolitics are bridge
  topics. Trade war mechanisms belong here; trade theory belongs
  there.
- **law-regulation**: international law belongs there. Geopolitical
  context for international law belongs here.
- **case-studies**: specific geopolitical events as case studies
  are bridge topics.
