# History

## Anchor

The study of past events, societies, and patterns of change across
human civilization. Encompasses political history (rise and fall of
states, empires, and governments), economic history (trade, industry,
financial systems), military history (conflicts, strategy, technology),
social history (daily life, class, gender, migration), and intellectual
history (ideas, ideologies, scientific revolutions). The anchor is
causality and context: how did we get here, what forces shaped the
present, and what patterns recur across time. Distinguished from
case-studies by its focus on broad historical forces and periods
rather than individual instances analyzed for business lessons.

## Scope

**In:** ancient civilizations and their legacies, medieval history,
the Renaissance and Enlightenment, the Industrial Revolution and
its consequences, colonialism and decolonization, world wars and
their aftermath, the Cold War period, economic history (trade
routes, financial crises, industrialization), history of science
and technology, history of political thought and ideology,
comparative history across civilizations, historiography and how
historical narratives are constructed, migration and demographic
history, history of specific regions and nations, religious history,
history of capitalism and markets, labor history, environmental
history.

**Out:** specific event analysis for business lessons (case-studies),
biographies of historical figures (notable-people), historical
fiction (pop-culture), anthropology of past societies
(anthropology), archaeological methods (science via anthropology),
historical philosophy (ethics-philosophy), geopolitical analysis
of current events (geopolitics), economic history as applied to
current investing (macro-micro), legal history of specific laws
(law-regulation).
