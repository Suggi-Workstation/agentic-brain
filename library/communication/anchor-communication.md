# Communication

## Anchor

The study of how humans convey, receive, and interpret information
through language, media, and symbolic systems. Encompasses rhetoric
(the art of persuasion), linguistics (the structure and evolution of
language), writing craft (composition, style, clarity), journalism
and media studies (how information is produced and distributed), and
interpersonal communication. The anchor is clarity and effectiveness:
how can ideas be transmitted accurately and persuasively from one
mind to another. This domain is foundational to the org's mission --
every agent produces written content, and every knowledge artifact
is an act of communication.

## Scope

**In:** rhetoric and persuasion (ethos, pathos, logos), argumentation
and logical fallacies, writing craft and style, copywriting and
technical writing, linguistics and language evolution, journalism
principles and media ethics, narrative structure and storytelling,
public speaking and presentation, visual communication and design
principles, information architecture and content design,
interpersonal communication and negotiation, propaganda and
misinformation analysis, media ecosystem and platform dynamics,
translation and cross-cultural communication, speechwriting,
editorial standards and fact-checking, cognitive science of
language processing, semiotics and meaning-making.

**Out:** literary criticism and analysis of specific works (books if
summarized), media business models (industries-sectors), communication
technology and infrastructure (technology), social media psychology
(psychology-behavior), freedom of speech law (law-regulation),
philosophy of language (ethics-philosophy), public relations as
industry practice, marketing strategy (industries-sectors).
