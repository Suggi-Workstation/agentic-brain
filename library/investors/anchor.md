# investors

## Anchor

The study of individual investors -- their biographies, investment
philosophies, track records, decision-making processes, and the
historical context in which they operated. This domain is about
WHO invested and HOW they thought, not about the abstract principles
they discovered. Focus on the person: their background, their
evolution as an investor, their major decisions and mistakes,
their letters and writings, and their lasting influence on capital
allocation. The line between "investor biography" and "investment
philosophy" is: if the topic is primarily about the PERSON, it
belongs here. If it is about the IDEAS they produced, it belongs
in value-investing.

## Scope

**In:** biographies of notable investors (Buffett, Munger, Graham,
Seth Klarman, Howard Marks, Peter Lynch, John Templeton, etc.),
investment partnership structures and histories, analysis of
specific investment decisions and their outcomes, investor letters
and annual reports as primary sources, evolution of an investor's
thinking over time, comparisons between investors' approaches,
the Graham-and-Doddsville community, investor partnerships and
their structures, track record analysis, investment firm histories.

**Out:** abstract investment principles (value-investing), valuation
techniques divorced from who used them (valuation-screening),
financial market mechanics (finance), company-specific analysis
without the investor context (industries-sectors), general business
biographies not focused on investing (notable-people), trading
psychology (psychology-behavior).

## Adjacent domains

- **value-investing**: investment philosophy belongs there. The
  people who developed and practiced that philosophy belong here.
- **notable-people**: general biographies belong there. Investors
  specifically belong here.
- **finance**: market mechanics belong there. How investors
  navigated those mechanics belongs here.
- **case-studies**: specific investment case studies can be bridge
  topics. If focused on the investor's decision process, here.
  If focused on the business outcome, there.
