# Accounting-financial-shenanigans

## Anchor

The detection and analysis of earnings manipulation, accounting
fraud, and financial statement deception. Named after Howard
Schilit's "Financial Shenanigans," this domain covers the specific
techniques companies use to distort financial reality -- revenue
recognition tricks, expense capitalization games, cash flow
misclassification, off-balance-sheet liabilities, and one-time
charge manipulation. The focus is forensic: how to spot the
manipulation, what red flags to look for, and what the corrected
numbers would show.

## Scope

**In:** revenue recognition shenanigans (premature, fictitious,
shifting), expense manipulation (capitalizing vs expensing,
cookie-jar reserves), cash flow misclassification, off-balance-
sheet entities, related-party transactions, non-GAAP metric abuse,
goodwill impairment games, acquisition accounting tricks,
restatement analysis, SEC enforcement cases, forensic accounting
methodology, red flag checklists, Beneish M-Score and similar
models, channel stuffing detection, bill-and-hold shenanigans.

**Out:** general accounting standards (finance), valuation adjustments
for honest accounting (valuation-screening), fraud beyond financial
statements, tax fraud, money laundering, audit procedures.

## Adjacent domains

- **finance**: accounting standards and normal financial reporting
  belong there. Deviations and manipulation belong here.
- **valuation-screening**: adjustments for honest but misleading
  accounting belong there. Adjustments for fraudulent accounting
  belong here.
- **law-regulation**: securities fraud prosecution belongs there.
  Detection methodology belongs here.
