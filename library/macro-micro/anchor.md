# macro-micro

## Anchor

The study of economic systems at both aggregate (macro) and
individual (micro) levels. Macroeconomics covers national and
global economic forces: GDP, inflation, unemployment, monetary
and fiscal policy, business cycles, interest rates, and trade.
Microeconomics covers individual decision-making: supply and
demand, price theory, market structures, incentives, and game
theory. This domain provides the economic context within which
businesses, investors, and policymakers operate.

## Scope

**In:** monetary policy and central banking, fiscal policy and
government spending, inflation and deflation dynamics, GDP
measurement and growth, unemployment and labor markets, business
cycle theory, interest rate mechanisms, trade and comparative
advantage, currency and exchange rates, supply and demand
elasticity, market structures (monopoly, oligopoly, competition),
price discovery, game theory applications, incentive design,
public goods and externalities, economic indicators, productivity
measurement, sovereign debt dynamics.

**Out:** investment philosophy (value-investing), financial market
mechanics (finance), portfolio strategy (portfolio-risk-management),
government regulation specifics (law-regulation), business
strategy (industries-sectors), economic history narratives
(case-studies), personal finance.

## Adjacent domains

- **finance**: market-level economic forces are bridge topics. When
  the focus is on the economy itself, here. When it is on market
  pricing, there.
- **geopolitics**: economic dimensions of international relations
  are bridge topics.
- **industries-sectors**: industry-specific economics belongs there.
  General economic principles belong here.
