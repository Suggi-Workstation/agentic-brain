# Engineering-infrastructure

## Anchor

The disciplined design, construction, operation, and maintenance of
physical systems under constraints of safety, cost, reliability,
energy, materials, and time. Encompasses civil, mechanical, electrical,
chemical, materials, industrial, and systems engineering together with
the infrastructure networks they create: transport, water, power,
communications facilities, buildings, manufacturing systems, and
public works. The anchor is engineered performance across the full
lifecycle, from requirements and design through operation, failure,
and renewal.

## Scope

**In:** engineering design methods, requirements and trade-offs,
systems engineering, reliability and safety engineering, civil and
structural engineering, mechanical systems, electrical power systems,
chemical and process engineering, materials engineering, industrial
engineering, manufacturing systems, construction methods, project and
asset lifecycle management, quality control, maintenance and failure
analysis, transport infrastructure, roads, railways, ports, airports,
water and wastewater systems, power grids, telecommunications physical
infrastructure, buildings and urban infrastructure, standards and
codes as engineering constraints, resilience, infrastructure finance
at the project-design level, and lifecycle cost analysis.

**Out:** pure natural laws and scientific discovery (science), software
and digital product technology (technology), industry economics and
competitive structure (industries-sectors), corporate management
(business-management-strategy), macroeconomic policy (macro-micro),
legal and regulatory doctrine (law-regulation), planetary and climate
mechanisms (earth-climate), and clinical treatment (health-medicine).
