# Political-science-public-policy

## Anchor

The study of domestic political power, government institutions,
collective decision-making, and the design, implementation, and
evaluation of public policy. Encompasses comparative political
systems, elections and parties, legislatures, executives, courts as
political institutions, bureaucracy, public administration, state
capacity, federalism, interest groups, and the political processes
that turn social demands into government action. The anchor is
institutional performance: how political rules distribute power and
how states convert authority into outcomes.

## Scope

**In:** comparative politics, democratic and authoritarian systems,
elections and voting systems, political parties, legislatures and
executives, bureaucracy and public administration, state capacity,
federalism and local government, interest groups and lobbying,
political economy, policy design and implementation, program
evaluation, taxation and spending as political choices, welfare-state
design, regulatory institutions, civil service, corruption and
accountability, public-sector incentives, institutional legitimacy,
citizen participation, social movements as political actors, and
methods for evaluating policy outcomes.

**Out:** relations between states and great-power strategy
(geopolitics), legal doctrine and judicial precedent (law-regulation),
normative theories of justice and legitimate authority
(ethics-philosophy), economic mechanisms of monetary and fiscal policy
(macro-micro), broad historical narrative (history), social structure
outside formal political institutions (sociology-demography), and
communication technique or media production (communication).
